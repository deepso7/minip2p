# TCP and circuit admission through memory reservations

Supplements ADRs 0012 and 0019. ADR 0019 gave QUIC a memory budget; TCP and circuits (secure-mux over Yamux) were bounded only by counts. A TCP transport could hold `max_connections` (1024) × Yamux `max_streams` (256) × the receive window of received data, and each session also pinned about 10 MiB of its own: twice the 4 MiB Yamux send cap (a queued write may keep a slice of its caller's payload, ADR 0012), the outbound buffer and the decoders. Circuits were capped only by NAT's `max_circuits`. Raising the Yamux window to what a real RTT needs (#361) multiplies all of that, so the budget and the new window land together (#357). We decided:

- **One memory budget per transport, in ADR 0019's terms.** Each `TcpTransport`, across its listeners, has a TCP memory budget (`TcpConfig::memory_budget_bytes`), and each `CircuitTransport`, across its circuits, a circuit memory budget (`CircuitLimits::memory_budget_bytes`). A relayed connection is charged at both layers: its bridge stream against the wrapped transport, its circuit session against the circuit budget. Refusals name the budget: `ResourceExhausted { resource: "TCP memory budget" }` and `"circuit memory budget"`.
- **The budget caps retained payload storage.** Queued sends (counted twice for slice pinning), receive windows, decoder backlogs and output backlogs are reserved. Container overhead, allocator slack, temporary copies and the provider's socket buffers are documented, not reserved.
- **Reservations follow ADR 0019.** A session reserves its fixed part at admission (`connection_reservation_bytes`: secure-mux's `fixed_reservation_bytes` plus the TCP outbound buffer and its one-message overshoot, or the circuit's one held bridge message) and releases it when teardown drops the session; there is no draining phase. A stream reserves its whole Yamux receive window when it opens or is admitted and holds it while empty. A closed stream with bytes not yet acknowledged keeps its whole reservation until it settles, also after its connection ended (#356's handover into `UnsettledConnections`); partial acknowledgement releases nothing.
- **Admission happens inside Yamux.** The owner lends its `MemoryLedger` (in `minip2p-transport`, next to `UnsettledConnections`; QUIC's budget is built on it too) to `handle_data_within` and `open_stream_within`. An inbound stream, whether opened by DATA+SYN or WINDOW_UPDATE+SYN, and including frames pipelined behind negotiation, is checked against it before any stream state or credit exists. A stream stays charged while it holds a slot (open, or closed but unsettled), and `take_settled_streams` reports each one that leaves exactly once, whatever ended it: a close, a reset, the final acknowledgement, a GoAway or a session failure. At teardown, `end_streams` hands over the unsettled counts and says how many other reservations to release.
- **Refusals are never queued.** A TCP dial or `open_stream` on either transport fails with `ResourceExhausted`; Yamux's `TooManyStreams` now maps to `ResourceExhausted { resource: "Yamux streams" }` instead of `PollError`. An inbound TCP connection is aborted at accept, as at `max_connections`. An inbound stream is reset, as past `max_streams`. Every failed construction rolls its reservation back.
- **Circuits hold a claim, not just a count.** Before it accepts a STOP or sends a HOP CONNECT, NAT asks the host to hold one more claim through `NatSwarm::hold_circuit_claims`, alongside the `max_circuits` slot; a claim is a circuit's fixed part reserved in the circuit ledger. A claim that does not fit refuses an inbound STOP with `RESOURCE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (status, then FIN) and fails a HOP leg with `ResourceExhausted { resource: "circuit memory budget" }`. Adoption with `BridgeAdoption::claimed` carries the claim into the circuit's reservation. After every call, the agent sets the host's claims to the circuits it is still negotiating, so a failed, cancelled or timed-out attempt releases its claim whichever path ended it, and adoption, having consumed one, needs nothing more. `adopt_bridge` also admits callers that bypass NAT, reserving for them, and stays idempotent: a repeat releases its claim.
- **Unbounded inputs get caps.** `adopt_bridge` rejects an `unsent_prefix` longer than one Noise message. Secure-mux feeds input to its decoders in batches of at most `MAX_INPUT_BATCH` (64 KiB), whatever a `TcpProvider` read or a bridge delivers, so decoder backlogs stay within the fixed part.
- **Defaults.** `YamuxConfig::default().receive_window` becomes 2,432 KiB, about twice the bandwidth-delay product of 100 Mbit/s at 100 ms, for TCP and circuits alike. It is the smallest window reaching 90% of a large window's goodput on the `yamux` sweep (`scripts/rtt-sweep.sh yamux`, `endpoint_rtt` over TCP at 100 Mbit/s, 100 ms, under the default budget): 10.95 MB/s, 94% of the reference's 11.69 MB/s, against 74% at 1,856 KiB and 100% from 3,072 KiB up. `DEFAULT_RECEIVE_WINDOW` stays the specification's 256 KiB initial credit; the rest is granted with the stream's first window update. `max_streams` stays 256 and the 4 MiB send cap stays. The budgets keep ADR 0019's floor of 64 sessions with one stream each:
  - TCP: 11.26 MiB fixed (11,809,083 bytes) plus a 2,432 KiB window is 13.6 MiB, and 64 of them need 873 MiB, so the default 1 GiB admits 75.
  - Circuits: 10.26 MiB fixed (10,760,507 bytes) plus the window is 12.6 MiB, and 64 of them need 809 MiB, so the default 896 MiB admits 70.

  Sessions get no fairness guarantee between them, as on QUIC.

- **Limits are checked at construction.** `TcpTransport::with_config` and `CircuitTransport::with_limits` return `InvalidLimit` when the Yamux invariants do not hold, the reservation arithmetic overflows, or the budget cannot fit one fixed part and one stream; TCP's zero outbound cap stays valid. Both `Endpoint` builders take the limits (`tcp_config`, `circuit_limits`) and report `EndpointOptionsError::InvalidLimit` / `PortableBuildError::InvalidLimit`.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Counting circuits against the TCP budget.** A relayed connection's circuit session lives in the circuit transport, whatever its bridge runs over (QUIC or TCP), so one budget per transport keeps each ledger next to what it charges.
- **A NAT-side count instead of a claim.** Counting pending circuits against the circuit budget without reserving would let streams on live circuits take the room between STOP and adoption, so an accepted circuit could still be refused at adoption.
- **A settled-stream event.** Events are dropped when a session fails; a count the owner drains cannot be lost, and is exactly-once by construction.
- **Any fairness quota per session, or a receive gate.** ADR 0012 rejected both.

## Consequences

- A TCP transport's retained payload is at most its TCP memory budget, and a circuit transport's at most its circuit budget, whatever the churn pattern.
- A reader that never acknowledges now exhausts the budget, not only the stream and connection slots, as on QUIC.
- A fresh Yamux stream now sends a window update with its open, granting the window past the initial 256 KiB.
- Relay-binary sizing (#381), FFI and SDK exposure (#382), an embedded small-budget profile (#383), the relay server's refusal status (#384) and window auto-tuning (#379) are separate work.
