# Std TCP provider: edge-triggered bookkeeping and per-poll budgets

`StdTcpProvider` is a `PollSource` (ADR 0013) over edge-triggered mio readiness, so it keeps its own record of what may be ready and never relies on an edge arriving twice.

- **Per-poll budgets, with a deferred-work flag.** Each socket reads at most `MAX_READS_PER_POLL` chunks and a poll accepts at most `MAX_ACCEPTS_PER_POLL` connections, so one fast peer or a connection flood cannot hold the poll open and starve other sockets and the host's timers. Interrupted accepts are charged to the budget too. Work left over stays marked (`readable` kept set) and sets `deferred_work`, which keeps the provider's deadline immediate: readiness will not announce it again. The accept budget is checked before `accept`, because a connection taken off the kernel queue and dropped is a peer's dial killed, not deferred. Listeners are serviced round-robin (`accept_cursor`), so the listener that led one poll goes last in the next and a flood on one cannot starve another.
- **Readable marks.** A newly adopted stream is marked readable, since data may have arrived before its registration could report an edge. A connecting socket is never marked readable (a stale edge for a recycled token would keep the provider due and spin a host until the connect settles); settling the connect probes a read instead. A read-closed socket is unmarked. Unattached, every open socket is treated as possibly readable; reading one that is not costs a `WouldBlock`.
- **Connect completion.** A refused or unreachable connect surfaces through `take_error` in `settle_connect`, not from `connect`. Writability can be reported before the handshake finishes, so `peer_addr` decides; every poll retries the connecting sockets rather than waiting for another edge, since under edge-triggered readiness the early edge may be the only one. Connects settle before reads, so a stream's `Connected` precedes its bytes. `send` on a connecting socket accepts nothing, even if the kernel already finished the connect, so no bytes leave before the provider has queued `Connected`.
- **Writable interest on demand.** A socket starts optimistic (`writable: true`, not watching); a partial or refused write turns write interest on, and the edge that reports room turns it off and queues `TcpEvent::Writable`. A dialed socket watches writability from the start, because that is how a connect reports completion, and stops once settled.
- **Stale tokens are harmless.** Tokens are reused (ADR 0013); an event for a freed token is ignored, and one for a recycled token only marks the new socket possibly ready. Attaching is all or nothing, so a failed attach can be retried from scratch. Listeners carry no readable flag because `accept` runs to `WouldBlock` on every poll.
- **Retiring.** A socket that has read the peer's FIN and shut its own write side is released at once (`retire_if_finished`, also after `close_write`), queueing `Closed`, rather than holding a descriptor for a stream the caller was told ended. `abort` discards any events already queued for the handle: the caller asked, so it is owed none.
- **Accept errors.** An accepted stream whose `adopt` fails is dropped (nothing names it yet) and the listener carries on. A failed `accept` (other than `WouldBlock` or `Interrupted`; `EMFILE`, say) ends that listener's turn and sets its `retry_at` to `ACCEPT_FAILURE_RETRY` (100 ms, matching mDNS's `MDNS_FAILURE_RETRY`) after the poll's `now`, which `next_deadline` reports: connections already queued in the backlog raise no further edge, so without the timer they would wait for an unrelated wake. The delay is fixed, not exponential; a successful or `WouldBlock` accept clears it. Each listener keeps its last reported failure, and both kinds are reported as capability failure events: `TcpEvent::AcceptFailed` when a listener starts failing or its failure changes (the whole error is compared, platform text included, because distinct errnos such as `EMFILE` and `ENOBUFS` share an `IoErrorKind`), nothing for a repeat, and `TcpEvent::AcceptRecovered` once a stream is accepted and adopted again. `TcpTransport` passes them on as `TransportEvent::ListenerError` (error boxed, so the enum keeps its size) and `ListenerRecovered`, and the swarm and Endpoint as `ListenerError` / `ListenerRecovered`.
