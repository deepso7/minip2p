# mDNS driver turns: bounded work per tick, and deadlines that let a device sleep

`MdnsDriver::tick` and `MdnsDriver::next_deadline` (crate `minip2p-mdns`) are shaped for two hosts at once: an OS host whose sockets wake it through a readiness poll, and a battery device running smoltcp in-process, which sleeps until the next deadline and gets nothing done between ticks. We decided:

- **Poll the carrier before reading and after sending.** An in-process stack moves nothing on its own: reading before driving it finds an empty link however much has arrived, and a datagram sent without driving afterwards sits until whatever wakes the host next.
- **Per-tick budgets count every datagram read, usable or not.** `receive_batch` counts oversized, off-link and unknown-interface datagrams toward `MAX_DATAGRAMS_PER_TICK`: they cost the same to read, and a flood of unusable ones must not buy an attacker unbounded work in one tick. `MdnsSockets::receive` rotates across interfaces starting where the last read stopped, so one talkative interface cannot starve a quiet one, and allocates nothing because it runs once per datagram.
- **Truncation is detected by filling the buffer.** The receive buffer is `MAX_DATAGRAM_BYTES + 1`; a datagram that fills it was truncated on the way in and is dropped rather than parsed. The smoltcp carrier copies as much as fits for the same reason, and drops `udp::RecvError::Truncated` outright (smoltcp never hands over part of a datagram).
- **What `next_deadline` combines.** Work left over by a tick's budget makes the next tick due now. Otherwise it is the earliest of the next interface refresh, the agent's next word, and the carrier's own deadline: an in-process stack's timers (retransmits of other sockets on a shared smoltcp stack included) would stall if the host slept past them. The smoltcp carrier reports "now" when a datagram is queued to send (it leaves only on the next poll) or already sits in a socket (delivered by the last poll, or by another user of a shared stack polling the interface, with no device input left to announce it). A deadline of zero delay is "now", not "never", and the sum saturates rather than wrapping into the past. While backing off from a failure, the deadline is the retry time alone.
- **Overdue deadlines report their past due time,** not "now" or a pushed-out time, so a host that ran late reads them as due.
- **The interface-refresh schedule starts at the first tick,** not at construction or some driver epoch: construction just enumerated the interfaces, so a host started late is not owed an immediate refresh, and is owed one exactly when the interval is up. Address changes reach mDNS only through that refresh (for example a DHCP lease landing or expiring on a smoltcp interface).
