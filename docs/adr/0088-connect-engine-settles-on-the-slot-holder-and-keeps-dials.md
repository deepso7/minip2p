# Connection engine: settling a race and keeping losing dials

The swarm settles a connection race the same way on both peers only once each has seen both connections (GLOSSARY: Connection replacement). `ConnectEngine` in `minip2p-rs` (`connect.rs`, `peer_connected`, `abort_one`, `RetainedDials`) is built around that, and around the swarm registering a whole transport batch before the engine sees any of it. We decided:

- **Settle on the slot holder, not the event's connection.** When `ConnectionEstablished` reaches the engine, a later connection of the same batch (another of our candidates winning the same-direction race by lower token) may already hold the peer's slot. The engine settles on the current slot holder at once instead of waiting for its queued `ConnectionReplaced`: waiting would let a `DialFailed` queued in between exhaust the attempt. The retired `conn_id` leaves the race without failing, so nothing aborts it. When the slot is already empty again, every connection of the batch is gone and settling on `conn_id` is as good as any (its close is queued). A circuit is the exception: the swarm has forgotten the address naming its relay, so there is no path to settle on, and it just leaves the race.
- **A replacement ends `old` and settles on `new`.** `ConnectionReplaced` returns any attempt provisional on `old` to its relay leg, exactly as a close would, even when `new` is already gone too; `new` then satisfies every pending attempt for the peer as a first establishment would, whichever side dialed it.
- **Kept dials stay up for the window.** After settling on a direct connection, the attempt's other direct dials stay open (the peer may already have accepted one and kept it) and are aborted only when the simultaneous-dial window ends. A dial that takes the slot leaves the kept set; a direct connection taking the slot restarts the swarm's window, so the kept dials' window restarts with it, and a later settle opens a new window for every kept dial. Whether the slot holder is our dial comes from the swarm (`SwarmCore::is_outbound`), since the attempt that made it, or its retained entry, may be gone by the time the engine sees it establish; engine bookkeeping is the fallback for a slot already empty again.
- **When the peer's dial wins as the lower peer, kept dials go at once.** Every dial of ours, including those kept from earlier attempts, now loses on both sides, so waiting out the window would only hold sockets open. A circuit settling also aborts pending dials at once.
- **Tombstones for `DialFailed` that cannot be retracted.** `abort_dial` cannot retract a `DialFailed` already queued in the batch. An abort that finds the dial gone (`Ok(false)`) or whose close fails (`Err`, dial restored) tombstones the id in `suppressed` so `observe` consumes the failure; on `Err` the dial is also vetoed so a restored dial cannot replace an existing connection. A dial aborted cleanly (`Ok(true)`) never fails, so its tombstone is dropped, and a tombstoned candidate that establishes drops its stale tombstone too, since no `DialFailed` will come for it.
