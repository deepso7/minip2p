# The peer example's ping channel survives hole-punch races

`examples/peer` (`minip2p-peer`) is the E2E harness for direct, relayed and hole-punched paths, so its dial loop (`run_dial` and its ping loop) and listener (`run_listen`) encode how an application has to treat a peer-scoped channel over connection-scoped lifecycle events:

- **A punch race replaces the connection under an open stream.** When both simultaneous-open DCUtR dials land, the swarm keeps the last connection, and a stream opened on the first dies moments after the upgrade. The dialer notices either through a failed `send_stream` or through the stream's own close event, and reopens the ping stream on the surviving connection; the reopen resends every outstanding sequence number, including the one whose send failed. A stream whose connection was replaced before its `StreamReady` arrived is reported back immediately so the caller retries on the new connection. Reopens are capped so a genuinely broken peer cannot loop the dialer forever.
- **Only the channel's own `StreamClosed` (or a failed send) ends the channel.** `ConnectionClosed` is connection-scoped and the channel is peer-scoped: during a QUIC connection replacement the old circuit can close just before the replacement direct connection is delivered. The old relay bridge has already been reset by the NAT agent and must never be touched again; pongs in flight on it are lost and resent on the new stream. When the counted run's last ping is out, remaining pongs can only arrive on the replacement, so its loss ends the run.
- **A plain direct dial must open its echo stream on the first try.** The dial's own queued `ConnectionEstablished` must not be read as a replacing punch connection (that would reset the stream and log a spurious retry); `examples/peer/tests/ping.rs` asserts no "direct channel setup failed" appears.
- **`PathUpgraded` can be printed by the setup waits.** The path can upgrade while the channel is being set up, so the ping loop reads the `State` snapshot, which is authoritative either way.
- **Ping pacing:** the next ping is scheduled from the actual send time, so slow event handling (notably a direct-channel upgrade) never turns missed ticks into a burst; a tick is skipped while the previous frame is still being accepted rather than queueing behind it. Teardown half-closes the stream so the listener echoes everything sent and then mirrors the close.
- **Listener echoes go through the Endpoint's write queue** (ADR 0030), which keeps echoes in order behind what a slow reader has not taken; a peer that sends more than the write budget without reading gets reset. The peer's FIN is mirrored behind every queued echo. A closed or replaced connection ends its streams without per-stream `StreamClosed` events, so the listener untracks all of that connection's streams on `ConnectionClosed`/`ConnectionReplaced`; echo streams live on the dialing peer's connection for direct streams and on the relay's for bridged circuits. Resetting an echo stream that is already gone is not an error.
- **Frame codec:** the 16-byte frame buffer compacts at most once per transport chunk and pops by advancing a cursor, rather than draining the front for every frame, which would repeatedly shift the unread suffix.
- **Dial setup (shared with `examples/chat`):** a `/p2p-circuit` target's relay leads the relay list and `--relay` adds a fallback; the dialer listens before dialing because its bound addresses are the local half of the DCUtR candidate set. A listener with WebTransport configured also serves browsers. The listener's stdout is the machine-readable event stream the E2E test scans.
